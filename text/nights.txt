# THE QUARANTINE — The night ladder, days 1-21. What the night does to the journal.
# Rewritten as lived moments from inside the house. No mechanic labels.
# §11.1 structure preserved; prose grounded in sensory detail and specific observation.

@night 1
You open the journal at the desk. The entry from this morning is there exactly—your
hand, the ink the same colour. You close the book and go to sleep.

@night 2
You turn to yesterday's entry. The time is wrong. Not badly wrong—off by an hour, like
you misread your watch. The ink is the same age as the paper around it. You read it
twice and the time is still wrong.

@night 3
The kitchen has become the hall. You read the entry back and that is what it says. But
you saw something yesterday that has no name in your vocabulary, and you cannot write
it. The page has a blank where a word should be, and no word exists to fill it.

@night 4
An entry appears in your hand, dated from three days ago. The handwriting is yours.
You do not remember writing it. You were asleep that night, or you were somewhere
else—the day is blurred. Two other entries have drifted in the meantime.

@night 5
One entry says the visitor came on Tuesday. The second entry, written on Tuesday, says
they did not come. You read them aloud to make sure you are reading correctly. Neither
has changed.

@night 6
The page numbers skip from 47 to 51. The space between is blank. And the entry you
locked with tape three nights ago—the one you wanted to keep—has drifted anyway. The
tape is still sealed around it.

@night 7
You open the journal to a page dated six days in the future. It is written in your
hand. The ink is fresh. You will not write these words for almost a week, and they are
here.

@night 8
Three separate entries have shifted since yesterday. The journal feels heavier. You
hold it at arm's length and then set it down.

@night 9
There is a full entry, in your hand, from a day you do not remember living. The words
cohere into a coherent account. Your handwriting. Your way of noting the time. You
have no memory of writing it.

@night 10
Page 63 ends and page 65 begins. Pages 64 is gone. Page 71 is gone. The numbering
skips like missing teeth. You turn the pages and count to make sure you are seeing
this correctly.

@night 11
One entry says it happened. Another entry, from that same day, says it did not. You
read them in sequence. You read them backwards. Both remain what they were written as.
The clock on the landing has stopped.

@night 12
Pages that were present yesterday are gone. Pages that were not here yesterday are
present. The numbering breaks and restarts itself. One entry is written in your hand
and is dated two days ago, but you did not write it two days ago.

@night 13
Multiple entries have shifted from where they were. Multiple entries now contradict
each other. You sit at the desk with the open journal. The day you just lived is on
the page in front of you. You do not recognize it.

@night 14
You wake knowing something occurred. The night was solid and real. There are no words
for it. The vocabulary does not have them. You sit at the desk and the page stays
empty because you have nothing to write. The silence is yours alone.

@night 15
The journal shifts in your hands when you open it. Several entries have drifted since
you last checked them. Some of them now contradict things you are certain of. You set
the book down without reading further.

@night 16
An entry is there, dated today, entirely in your hand. You have no memory of writing
it. The words read as something you might have said, if you were speaking yourself to
someone else.

@night 17
Two entries now describe the same moment differently. You remember seeing this moment
replayed to you. The entry in your hand is what you remember. The replay was wrong.
You will never tell anyone what you remember, because no one else will believe there is
a difference.

@night 18
Pages vanish between one night and the next. Other pages shift. The numbering is
ruined. You spend an hour flipping backwards through the journal, trying to find a
pattern. By dawn you stop.

@night 19
Your own words are returned to you wrong. Some entries replay exactly as you wrote
them. Others are played back with details you know you never wrote. You lie in the bed
and watch. The page stays empty because you are watching, not writing.

@night 20
Entries appear that do not belong to the structure of your days. Earlier entries now
contradict them in ways that cannot be resolved. The replays continue underneath. You
close the journal without finishing.

@night 21
The final night shifts what remains. Multiple entries change. You turn the pages in the
morning light. By the time you reach the end, you are reading the record of someone
who lived here, in this house, in these days. You cannot be certain it was you.
