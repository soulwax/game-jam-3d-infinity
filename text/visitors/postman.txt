# The postman — Ken Dalgleish. Days 2, 7, 12, 17. Post is one of the few things
# that still crosses the boundary, so he is the regular evidence that an outside
# exists. He knows every house on the row by what comes through its door rather
# than by who lives behind it. Harmless gossip, no malice, never a threat.
# Branch encoding per the wave brief §2: full.N / compressed.N, contiguous from 1.
# The previous tenant's post (§24.1) is an administrative nuisance to him and he
# never treats it as anything else. He does not resolve it and never learns a name.

@visitor postman

# --- Day 2 — he introduces himself into the routine. Mrs Ayling at nine and Mrs
# Pethick at ten are named the way a postman names them: by their post.
@day 2
@arrival 10 0
@tier full.1
Ken. Ken Dalgleish. I've had this walk since before they put the new boxes in.
@claim who Ken Dalgleish
@tier full.2
There's still post coming for the one before you. I bring it till somebody tells me not to.
@tier full.3
Nine takes two papers and ten takes none. I know this street by what goes through the door.
@tier full.4
Mrs Pethick's had a card off her sister every week since {the spring|the summer}. Never misses.

# --- Day 2, off. Same gossip register, over-specific about a bureaucratic
# word he'd never actually use, vague about how long he's really had the walk.
@tier off.1
Ken. I've had this walk a good while now — I couldn't tell you exactly how long.
@tier off.2
There's still post for the one before you. It's on my record to keep bringing it.
@claim object the record
@cue off-key

# --- Day 7 — the week's decisive day, and the post is what changed. His explanation
# is reasonable and is the only one anybody offers: it was held, and now it isn't.
@day 7
@arrival 10 0
@tier full.1
It's all come at once today. They'd been holding the lot at the office and now they've not.
@claim object a delivery
@tier full.2
One delivery a day from here on, and it'll be the morning. That's the order, it's not mine.
@tier full.3
Nothing for half the row and four for you. That's how it falls when it's been stacked up.
@tier full.4
Two more for the one before you. I'd open them, myself. It's your door they came to.

# --- Day 7, off. The held-and-released explanation intact, but the verb has
# slipped: what was held is now, in his telling, something sent back and forth.
@tier off.1
It's all come at once today, or near enough all of it. They'd had it a good while.
@tier off.2
One a day from here on. Or that's the order — I've had it returned to me twice already.
@claim verb returned
